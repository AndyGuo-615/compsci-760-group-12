The metadata audit revealed substantial differences in acquisition characteristics across blood-group classes. Although the overall hand distribution was approximately balanced (49.0% left and 51.0% right), the distribution varied considerably by blood group. For example, 88.3% of A+ images were from the left hand, whereas 92.3% of B− images were from the right hand. Similarly, finger-type distributions differed substantially across classes; for example, thumb images accounted for 51.7% of A+ and 50.9% of B− samples, while little-finger images accounted for 40.5% of AB+ samples and 37.9% of B+ samples.

Subject identifiers ranged from 1 to 600, with all 600 subjects appearing in multiple blood-group classes. Therefore, subject ID does not uniquely determine blood group. Nevertheless, subjects contributed different combinations and numbers of blood-group-labelled images, meaning that subject identifiers may still contain information about the dataset composition. These findings provide evidence of potential dataset-level and acquisition-related confounding and motivate the use of metadata controls alongside the main fingerprint classification experiments.

Importantly, these metadata associations do not imply that blood group is biologically determined by hand or finger position. Rather, they indicate that the construction and acquisition structure of this dataset is not independent of the class labels.
